🎯 THE CHALLENGE

Build a folder-based cartographer that can walk a body of work and leave a map a later reader can wander. Show what that map is.

Your cartographer should:
- Navigate through a collection of work organized in folders
- Create a navigable map/index of that content
- Serve audiences including both AI systems and human readers
- Produce a consistent map format regardless of audience type

🗂️ THE METHODOLOGY

If this is your first comp, welcome. Here's what you need to know:

This week (and every week) you're learning interpretable context methodology. Folders as architecture. Each file does one job well.

Your cartographer is a folder with these essentials:

📄 identity.md (who the cartographer is, what bodies of work it maps)  
📐 rules.md (how it creates maps)  
💬 examples.md (what good maps look like)  
📚 reference/ (templates, frameworks, standards the cartographer uses)  
📖 README.md (how to use it)  

Drop the folder into a Claude project. Claude becomes the cartographer. Reusable. Shareable. Portable.

🔥 THE ANGLE THIS WEEK  

Mapmaking feels disorienting at first. But it's essential. A map helps future audiences — human and AI — understand complex bodies of work.

Your cartographer isn't a summarizer. It doesn't flatten or hide structure. It reveals the terrain. It shows what's where, how pieces connect, and what a later reader needs to know to navigate.

Your rules.md teaches the cartographer what a useful map looks like for *your* specific domain and audience.

💼 WHY THIS LANDS ON YOUR RESUME

Navigation is leverage. Companies hire people specifically to help teams find what they need in large codebases, documentation, or project histories.

"I built a cartographer that maps [specific type of work] for [specific audience]" demonstrates systems thinking, domain expertise, and the ability to make complexity navigable. 🚀

Win or lose, you walk out with something you'd be proud to link from your LinkedIn.

📐 THE BAR

Someone with no context should point your cartographer at a body of work and get back a map that actually helps them navigate it.

If the output is a generic summary or outline, you've missed the assignment.
