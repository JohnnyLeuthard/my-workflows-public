# 🏆 WEEKLY COMP #12: THE AUDITOR 🏆

💵 PRIZE: $500 CASH 💵

One winner takes it.

🎯 THE CHALLENGE

Build a folder-based AI auditor that checks something against a specific standard and reports where it falls short.

📏 PICK YOUR STANDARD

The standard has to be real. It has to actually exist in the world — a published spec, a regulation, a style guide, a compliance framework, a documented best-practice checklist.

Not your personal judgment. Not "what good looks like to me." A standard someone else could look up and verify you applied correctly.

That constraint is what makes the audit useful to an industry instead of just to you.

🗂️ THE METHODOLOGY

If this is your first comp, welcome. Here's what you need to know:

This week (and every week) you're learning interpretable context methodology. Folders as architecture. Each file does one job well.

Your auditor is a folder with these essentials:

📄 identity.md (who the auditor is, what it audits, against which standard)  
📐 rules.md (how it checks and how it reports findings)  
💬 examples.md (what a good audit report looks like)  
📚 reference/ (the standard itself, checklists, clause mappings)  
📖 README.md (how to use it)  

Drop the folder into a Claude project. Claude becomes the auditor. Reusable. Shareable. Portable.

🔥 THE ANGLE THIS WEEK

An auditor measures against an external yardstick.

The output isn't an opinion — it's a finding. Each one should point at the thing being audited, name the specific requirement it misses, and cite where that requirement comes from. "This section is weak" is not an audit finding. "Section 4 omits the retention period required by clause 7.2" is.

Your rules.md is where you teach the AI to check rather than to judge.

📝 HOW TO SUBMIT

Drop a public GitHub repo link in the comments, plus 2-3 sentences on what your auditor checks, which standard it checks against, and who it's for.
